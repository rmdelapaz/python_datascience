Saving Figures

75 min alone
By the end you can
  • Save a figure with fig.savefig() and predict its size in pixels from figsize and dpi
  • Choose between raster formats (PNG) and vector formats (SVG, PDF) for a given destination
  • Explain why text looks tiny or huge in a saved chart, and fix it with the figure size
  • Control the saved area and background with bbox_inches, facecolor and transparent
  • Write one function that applies a house style and saves for web, slides or print, then use it in a loop that closes each figure
Key ideas
  • The sheet and the scanner. figsize is the sheet of paper, in inches. dpi is the scanner. pixels = inches x dpi, once for the width and once for the height.
  • Say which figure. fig.savefig(path) saves the figure you name. plt.savefig() saves whatever pyplot thinks is current, which can be an empty one.
  • The format comes from the extension. .png is a grid of pixels (raster). .svg and .pdf are drawing instructions (vector) and stay sharp at any size.
  • Text is sized in points, and a point is 1/72 of an inch on the sheet. A smaller sheet makes the same text look bigger. Raising the dpi never does.
  • Destinations. Web or email: PNG, about 110 dpi. A 16:9 slide: PNG at 9.6 x 5.4 inches and 200 dpi (1,920 x 1,080). Print or anything that will be resized: PDF or SVG. Never JPEG for charts.
  • bbox_inches="tight" stops labels being cut off and changes the saved size. With layout="constrained" you rarely need it.
  • transparent=True removes the background only. Black text on a dark slide vanishes. A solid facecolor="white" is the safe default.
  • Presets and one save function. Put sizes, dpi and formats in one dictionary; create the figure at the right size before drawing; save through one function.
  • Create, draw, save, close. In a loop, plt.close(fig) after every save. PdfPages puts many figures in one PDF.
  • Do not trust, check. plt.imread(path).shape is (rows, columns, channels). Then open the file at the size it will really be shown.
Worked example

The intranet home page has a banner slot exactly 1,200 pixels wide and 300 high. Work backward: 8 inches x 150 dpi = 1,200 and 2 inches x 150 dpi = 300. Create the figure at that size, draw, save, close, check.

from pathlib import Path
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import pandas as pd

daily = pd.read_csv("data/daily_sales.csv", parse_dates=["date"])
weekly = daily.set_index("date")["visitors"].resample("W").sum()

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(8, 2), layout="constrained")
ax.plot(weekly.index, weekly.values, color="#667eea")
ax.set_title("All stores: visitors per week", loc="left")

Path("charts").mkdir(exist_ok=True)
banner = Path("charts") / "visitors_banner.png"
fig.savefig(banner, dpi=150, facecolor="white")     # 8 x 150 by 2 x 150
plt.close(fig)

print(plt.imread(banner).shape)
print("open figures:", len(plt.get_fignums()))
(300, 1200, 4)
open figures: 0

300 rows, 1,200 columns, 4 values per pixel (red, green, blue, alpha). Zero open figures, because the figure was closed after saving. The same 1,200 x 300 pixels from a 12 x 3 inch sheet at 100 dpi would have a title two thirds the size.

4.1

Descriptive Statistics I: Center and Spread

85 min alone
By the end you can
  • Compute the mean, median and mode of a column with pandas and say what each one measures
  • Recognize when the mean misleads, and choose the median instead, using Pinecrest's skewed order totals
  • Compute range, variance, standard deviation and interquartile range (IQR), and explain each in the units of the data
  • Explain the difference between dividing by n and by n - 1, and predict which one NumPy and pandas use by default
  • Pair the right measure of center with the right measure of spread for a given column
Key ideas
  • Two questions about any column. Center: what is typical? Spread: how much does it vary? One number is never enough.
  • Mean = sum / count: the balance point. It uses every value, multiplies back to the total, and is pulled by extremes.
  • Median = the middle value of the sorted list. Half the values are below it. It is robust: an extreme value cannot move it far.
  • Mode = the most common value. The only center for categories. .mode() returns a Series because ties happen; take [0] for one value.
  • The mean of a True/False column is a proportion. (totals < totals.mean()).mean() is the share of orders below the mean: 0.626.
  • Right-skewed data (a long tail to the right, like anything about money) has its mean above its median. The gap is information, not an error.
  • Variance = the average squared distance from the mean. Standard deviation (SD) = its square root, back in the data's own units: "a typical distance from the mean".
  • n or n - 1? NumPy divides by n (ddof=0, a population). pandas divides by n - 1 (ddof=1, a sample). If unsure, ddof=1.
  • IQR = Q3 - Q1, the width of the middle half of the data. Robust, like the median.
  • Two standard pairs. Symmetric data: mean and SD. Skewed data, or data that may hold errors: median and IQR. A histogram tells you which you have.
  • Missing values. pandas skips them (print .count() next to len()); np.mean returns nan without an error.
Worked example

Two columns from the product catalog, summarized the same way: the star rating (1 decimal) and the number of reviews.

import pandas as pd

products = pd.read_csv("data/products.csv")
for column in ["rating", "review_count"]:
    values = products[column]
    iqr = values.quantile(0.75) - values.quantile(0.25)
    gap = (values.mean() - values.median()) / values.median() * 100
    print(column, "| n", values.count())
    print(f"  mean {values.mean():.2f}  median {values.median():.2f}")
    print(f"  mean vs median {gap:+.1f}%")
    print(f"  SD {values.std():.2f}  IQR {iqr:.2f}")
rating | n 234
  mean 4.14  median 4.10
  mean vs median +0.9%
  SD 0.39  IQR 0.50
review_count | n 242
  mean 70.45  median 39.00
  mean vs median +80.7%
  SD 88.57  IQR 75.75

Read the gap first (how far the mean sits above the median, as a percentage of the median). For rating the mean and the median almost agree (the mean is 0.9% higher), so there is no long tail: report mean and SD, "ratings average 4.14 with an SD of 0.39". For review_count the mean is 81% above the median: a few products have hundreds of reviews. Report median and IQR, "a typical product has 39 reviews; the middle half have between 14 and about 90". Notice also n: 8 products have no rating yet, and pandas left them out without saying so.

Glossary

  • dpi: dots per inch: how many pixels the saved image has for each inch of the figure.
  • figsize: the size of the figure in inches, (width, height). The sheet of paper.
  • Raster image: a grid of colored pixels with a fixed resolution (PNG, JPEG).
  • Vector image: a list of drawing instructions that stays sharp at any size (SVG, PDF).
  • Point: the unit of font sizes and line widths: 1/72 of an inch on the sheet.
  • Bounding box: the area of the figure that is saved. bbox_inches="tight" fits it to what is drawn, which changes the size.
  • facecolor: the figure's background color. transparent=True removes it.
  • Preset: a named set of saved settings (size, dpi, format) for one destination.
  • Manifest: a small table listing every file you produced, used to verify an export.
  • Center (central tendency): what is typical in a column: mean, median or mode.
  • Spread (variability): how much the values vary: range, variance, SD or IQR.
  • Mean: the sum divided by the count; the balance point of the data.
  • Median: the middle value of the sorted data; half the values are below it.
  • Mode: the most common value; the only center for categories.
  • Robust: barely affected by extreme values. The median and the IQR are robust; the mean and the SD are not.
  • Right-skewed: having a long tail of large values to the right; the mean sits above the median.
  • Range: the largest value minus the smallest.
  • Variance: the average squared deviation from the mean.
  • Standard deviation (SD): the square root of the variance, in the data's own units; a typical distance from the mean.
  • ddof: "delta degrees of freedom": the number subtracted from n before dividing. NumPy's default is 0, pandas's is 1.
  • Population / sample: all of the group you care about / a part of it used to estimate the whole.
  • Quartiles, IQR: Q1 and Q3 have a quarter and three quarters of the values below them; the IQR is Q3 - Q1, the width of the middle half.
