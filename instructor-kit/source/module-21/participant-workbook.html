Lesson

8.1

The Pinecrest Report

By the end of today you can
  • State your main result as one sentence with an estimate, an interval, a test and an effect size
  • Try to break your own result with one robustness check
  • Draw one figure that carries your answer on its own
  • Present your answer in three minutes, and finish the report within a week
Key ideas
  • Compare like with like. Before you believe a difference, ask what else differs between the groups: the season, the time each customer had, the weekdays, the products.
  • Fit the method to the data. Two proportions: a z-test with Cohen's h. A skewed amount: medians with a bootstrap interval, means alongside. Several groups: one overall test first, then a small number of planned pairs, adjusted.
  • Five parts in every claim: the estimates, the difference, the 95% interval, the test, the effect size.
  • An honest null is a full answer. Say what the interval rules out: "no difference larger than about X".
  • Words about cause ("causes", "drives", "because") only where the design supports them.
  • One figure, one message. A title that states the finding, axes with units, error bars named in the caption, saved from code.
  • If it does not rerun from a fresh start, it is not finished.
Worked example: a strong sentence for a null result

A practice question (not a capstone question): do customers who opted in to marketing recommend Pinecrest more? "Promoter" means a recommend score of 9 or 10.

from statsmodels.stats.proportion import confint_proportions_2indep, proportions_ztest

survey = pd.read_csv("data/survey.csv")
customers = pd.read_csv("data/customers.csv")
t = survey.merge(customers[["customer_id", "marketing_opt_in"]], on="customer_id",
                 validate="one_to_one").dropna(subset=["recommend"])
t["promoter"] = t["recommend"] >= 9
g = t.groupby("marketing_opt_in")["promoter"].agg(["sum", "count"])
yes, no = g.loc[True], g.loc[False]
p1, p0 = yes["sum"] / yes["count"], no["sum"] / no["count"]
z, p = proportions_ztest([yes["sum"], no["sum"]], [yes["count"], no["count"]])
low, high = confint_proportions_2indep(yes["sum"], yes["count"], no["sum"], no["count"])
h = 2 * np.arcsin(np.sqrt(p1)) - 2 * np.arcsin(np.sqrt(p0))
print(f"Promoters (recommend 9 or 10): {p1:.1%} of opted-in customers (n={yes['count']}) "
      f"against {p0:.1%} of the others (n={no['count']}), a difference of {(p1 - p0) * 100:+.1f} points "
      f"(95% CI {low * 100:+.1f} to {high * 100:+.1f}; z-test p = {p:.2f}; h = {h:.3f}).")
Promoters (recommend 9 or 10): 32.3% of opted-in customers (n=1020) against 31.3% of the others (n=726), a difference of +1.0 points (95% CI -3.5 to +5.4; z-test p = 0.66; h = 0.021).

Every part is there: two rates with their group sizes, the difference, the interval, the test and the effect size. The interval runs from about 3.5 points lower to 5.4 points higher: the data cannot tell the groups apart, and they rule out a difference much larger than 5 points. That is the sentence a report would carry, word for word.

Notes

Glossary

  • Unit of analysis: what one row of your analysis table is: a store-day, a customer, a product.
  • Fair comparison: groups that differ in the thing you care about and, as far as you can arrange, in nothing else.
  • Follow-up window: a fixed period after an event (for example 180 days) in which an outcome is counted, the same for everyone.
  • Difference in differences: the change in an affected group minus (or divided by) the change in an unaffected group over the same period.
  • Effect size: how big a difference is, on a scale that does not depend on sample size: Cohen's d for means, h for proportions.
  • Multiple comparisons: running many tests; some will look significant by chance, so plan the main one and adjust or report the rest.
  • Honest null: a carefully measured "no clear difference", reported with the size of difference the data rule out.
  • Robustness check: repeating the main comparison with another reasonable definition or subset to see whether it holds.
  • Reproducible: the notebook gives the same numbers after Restart and Run All, on another machine, from relative paths.
