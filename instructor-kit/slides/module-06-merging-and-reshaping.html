2.5

Merging and Joining

Connect tables by their keys, and notice when a merge loses rows or multiplies them.

2.5 · Why it matters

Each fact is stored once, so questions need several tables

  • An order says customer 12673. The name, state and tier are in customers.csv
  • What was bought is in order_items.csv. Its category is in products.csv
  • 'Revenue by category' needs three tables at once
  • A merge can quietly lose rows or multiply them. Neither raises an error
2.5 · Mental model

The claim ticket

  • Leave skis for tuning, get a numbered ticket. The number connects you to the skis
  • A key is that number: a column that appears in both tables
  • What if a ticket has no match? how= decides
  • What if a ticket matches more than once? The rows multiply
SAY IT Count before. Count after.

Four kinds of join

left = pd.DataFrame({"sku": ["A", "B", "C"], "qty": [2, 1, 5]})

for how in ["inner", "left", "right", "outer"]:

2.5 · Common mistakes

Three mistakes everyone makes once

  1. The default inner join as an accidental filter. 739 orders gone, no warning. how="left".
  2. Summing order_total after merging with the lines. $11.0 million instead of $4.0 million.
  3. Keys that do not line up. Different dtype, different name, wrong validate.
WATCH The first two raise no error at all.

2.6

Pivot Tables and Reshaping

Turn rows into a grid a person can read, and a grid back into rows a program can use.

2.6 · Why it matters

The data you load and the table you present are different shapes

  • The operations manager does not want 20,302 rows. She wants one small grid
  • 'Which store grew fastest?' wants the two years side by side
  • 'Average revenue per month across stores' wants one column of numbers to group
  • A grid looks authoritative. Every cell hides the rows behind it
2.6 · Mental model

The logbook and the scoreboard

  • Logbook: one event per line. Long, narrow, easy for a program. Tidy
  • Scoreboard: a summary in a grid. Short, wide, easy for a person
  • pivot_table() turns a logbook into a scoreboard
  • melt() turns a scoreboard back into a logbook
SAY IT rows? columns? values? how to combine?

2.6 · Common mistakes

Three mistakes everyone makes once

  1. No aggfunc. The cells are means, and the title says revenue.
  2. pivot() on a logbook. ValueError: duplicate entries. Use pivot_table.
  3. Trusting the grid. Alphabetical months, zeros for 'no data', a total that is short.
WATCH Check one cell by hand. Reconcile the corner with the source.
