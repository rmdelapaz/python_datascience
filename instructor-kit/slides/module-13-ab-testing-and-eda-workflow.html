A/B Testing

From the plan written before launch to the memo that says "ship it".

4.6 · Why it matters

Observational data has a permanent weakness

  • Last session: rain and visitors. Rain also comes with seasons.
  • The groups you compare differ in more ways than the one you care about.
  • An experiment removes that by design: same weeks, a coin flip decides.
  • It does not make the statistics optional.

4.6 · Mental model

The coin does the controlling

  • Flip a coin for each person: room A or room B.
  • Every kind of person splits about evenly, including kinds you never measured.
  • The rooms are alike in everything except what you do next.
  • A difference afterward: the treatment, or chance. You know how to judge chance.
4.6 · Design before data

The plan, in six lines

  1. Unit: a visitor who reaches checkout
  2. Primary metric: conversion per visitor
  3. Guardrail: order value, revenue per visitor
  4. Hypotheses: two-sided, alpha = 0.05
  5. Size: computed first, then fixed
  6. Decision rule: ship if conversion is higher and the guardrail holds

4.6 · Common mistakes

Three ways to get a clean, wrong number

  • The raw difference as effect size: effect_size=0.011 → 129,733 per arm
  • Rates instead of counts in proportions_ztest → p = 0.98
  • Two p-values on opposite sides of 0.05 → "the segments differ"
FIX Convert with proportion_effectsize · pass counts · test the difference

4.7 · Why it matters

Real work does not hand you the right tool

  • Someone sends a file and a vague question.
  • What first? What next? When do you stop?
  • Without a routine: charts until something looks interesting.
  • That is slow, and a reliable way to fool yourself.
4.7 · Mental model

The detective at the scene: seven steps

  1. Questions: who wants to know what?
  2. Structure: what is one row?
  3. Quality: missing, impossible, unrepresentative?
  4. One variable at a time
  5. Two variables together
  6. Segments: the same for every group?
  7. Findings and caveats, in plain sentences

4.7 · Common mistakes

Tidy numbers, quietly wrong

  • dropna() on everything: 1,800 responses → 295
  • fillna(0) on ratings: staff 4.02 → 2.67
  • A merge that multiplies rows: 1,800 → 9,230, no message
  • The best of 55 comparisons, reported as if planned
FIX subset= · leave gaps as gaps · validate= · count, correct, say "exploratory"
